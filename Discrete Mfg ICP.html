What is discrete manufacturing?

The production of distinct, countable units assembled from a bill of materials, such as an engine, a circuit board or a CT scanner. Each unit can be serialised or lot-tracked, reworked or recalled individually. Supply chains are BOM-driven, multi-tier and sensitive to a single missing component, which makes material availability, schedule adherence and tier-n supplier risk the central challenges.

Persona 01 · Economic Buyer

Chief Supply Chain Officer / SVP Supply Chain

Their world

Owns end-to-end supply chain across plants, suppliers and customers. Wants to get ahead of supply risk instead of reacting to it. Multi-tier, BOM-driven supply chains mean one late component can stop a line, yet shortages are often found when the line stops rather than weeks earlier. Planning, execution and supplier data sit in separate ERPs and tools, so every decision starts with reconciliation.

What they care about

  • Getting ahead of supply risk: shortages and supplier issues seen weeks before the line is hit
  • Hitting customer OTIF commitments while carrying less inventory
  • Tier-n visibility beyond direct suppliers
  • Reducing E&O and working capital tied up in inventory
  • Turning AI investment into decisions planners actually use

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • OTIF

    On Time In Full: share of customer orders delivered on the committed date and in the full quantity.

    Orders on time and in full ÷ total ordersHeadline customer service KPI
  • Inventory Turns / DIO

    Turns: how often inventory is consumed and replaced per year. DIO: days of inventory held.

    Turns = COGS ÷ avg inventory; DIO = 365 ÷ turnsWorking capital lever
  • E&O Inventory

    Excess and obsolete stock: inventory above forecast need or no longer usable after design or demand changes.

    E&O value ÷ total inventory valueECOs and end-of-life drive it
  • Cash-to-Cash Cycle

    Days between paying suppliers and collecting cash from customers.

    DIO + DSO − DPOBoard-level working capital KPI
  • Tier-n Visibility

    Share of critical parts where sub-tier suppliers (tier 2 and beyond) are mapped and monitored.

    Critical parts with mapped sub-tiers ÷ critical partsMost disruptions start below tier 1

Persona 02 · Business Champion

VP Manufacturing / VP Operations

Their world

Owns plant performance and the production schedule. Needs schedules to run so customer commitments are not missed. Plant data lives in SCADA, PLCs, historians and MES that differ site by site, so comparing lines or plants is slow and manual. Unplanned downtime and material shortages are the main reasons schedules slip.

What they care about

  • Schedules that run, so commitments are not missed
  • Cross-site view of OEE and downtime without manual data stitching
  • Fewer line stoppages from material shortages and asset failures
  • Moving from reactive to predictive maintenance
  • Linking shop-floor data to supply and demand plans

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • OEE

    Overall Equipment Effectiveness: how much of planned production time is truly productive.

    Availability × performance × qualityWorld-class is often quoted around 85%
  • Schedule Adherence

    Share of planned production orders completed on the scheduled day, in the scheduled quantity and sequence.

    Orders built to schedule ÷ orders scheduledMissed schedules break customer commitments
  • Unplanned Downtime

    Production hours lost to unplanned stoppages: breakdowns, material shortages, quality holds.

    Unplanned stop hours ÷ planned production hoursLine-down events are the most expensive
  • JIT / JIS Delivery Performance

    Share of just-in-time / just-in-sequence deliveries to the line on time and in the right sequence.

    On-time, in-sequence deliveries ÷ totalMisses stop the line
  • First Pass Yield

    Share of units that pass all quality checks first time without rework or scrap.

    Good units first time ÷ units startedRework hides cost and capacity

Persona 03 · Business Champion

Head of Supply Planning / Materials Planning

Their world

Leads MRP and supply plans across plants and thousands of BOM components. Planners spend much of the week clearing MRP exceptions and reconciling data between ERPs, so shortages are spotted late and expedites follow. Multiple ERP instances mean there is no single view of inventory or open supply.

What they care about

  • Fewer, better-prioritised MRP exceptions
  • One view of inventory and open supply across ERP instances
  • Clear-to-build visibility weeks ahead
  • Right-sized safety stock and less E&O
  • Fewer expedites

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • MRP Exceptions

    Volume of reschedule-in/out, expedite and shortage messages MRP generates that planners must action.

    Open exception messages per planner per weekHigh counts = planners firefighting
  • Material Availability

    Share of production orders released with all BOM components on hand (clear-to-build).

    Orders clear to build ÷ orders due for releaseLeading indicator for schedule adherence
  • Forecast Accuracy (WMAPE)

    Volume-weighted forecast error, so high-runner parts count more than slow movers.

    Σ|actual − forecast| ÷ Σ actualLumpy, long-tail demand is common
  • Inventory Turns / DIO

    Turns: how often inventory is consumed and replaced per year. DIO: days of inventory held.

    Turns = COGS ÷ avg inventory; DIO = 365 ÷ turnsWorking capital lever
  • Service-parts Fill Rate

    Share of aftermarket service-part orders filled from stock first time.

    Lines filled from stock ÷ lines orderedDrives aftermarket margin and uptime

Persona 07 · Influencer

Chief Financial Officer / VP Finance

Their world

Owns the business case and working capital. Sees supply chain failures as cost: expedited freight, premium buys, E&O write-downs and missed revenue. Wants inventory and margin decisions grounded in numbers finance trusts.

What they care about

  • Avoiding expedited freight and premium buys
  • Lower working capital and E&O
  • Margin protection when material costs move
  • A single, trusted number set with operations
  • Clear payback on transformation spend

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Expedited Freight Cost

    Premium freight spend to recover late supply or late customer shipments.

    Expedite spend ÷ total freight spendA visible symptom of late signals
  • Working Capital

    Cash tied up in inventory, receivables and payables.

    Inventory + receivables − payablesCFO-owned target
  • E&O Inventory

    Excess and obsolete stock: inventory above forecast need or no longer usable after design or demand changes.

    E&O value ÷ total inventory valueECOs and end-of-life drive it
  • Gross Margin

    Revenue left after cost of goods sold.

    (Revenue − COGS) ÷ revenuePricing and material cost both move it
