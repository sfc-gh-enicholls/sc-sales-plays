Why now

Biologics, cell and gene therapies and specialty products push more volume through tight temperature ranges, while serialisation rules and inspections demand faster evidence. QMS, MES, LIMS, ERP, serialisation and cold chain data still sit apart, so excursions and trace requests start with manual reconciliation.

Quality & Batch Release

Every batch on hold is stock you cannot ship. The bottleneck is usually the evidence, not the lab.

Batch Right-First-Time

Share of batches released without deviation investigation, rework or reprocessing.

batches released first time ÷ batches completedBy product, site and line · Owner: Head of Quality / QA Director
Released without investigation · Owner: Head of Quality / QA Director
Typical: 85–92%Target: 97%+ established products

Illustrative: a site completing 400 batches a year moves from 90% to 95% right-first-time. That is 40 − 20 = 20 fewer investigations; at 10 extra hold days each, 200 batch hold-days avoided.

Process parameters, in-process checks, LIMS results and deviation history joined by batch, so at-risk batches are flagged before release hold.

Cold Chain & Delivery

Illustrative: 40,000 cold chain shipments a year at 1.0% is 400 excursions. Halving to 0.5% avoids 200; if 10% would have been written off at €150k each, that is 200 × 10% × €150k = €3M a year.

Forecast error · Owner: Head of Demand Planning

Illustrative: forecast 1,000 packs, actual 800. Error = |800 − 1,000| ÷ 800 = 25%. High error drives both stockouts and near-expiry stock.
