What is CPG?

The manufacturing and distribution of branded, frequently purchased consumer products sold through retailers. CPG companies use process manufacturing (batches, recipes, formulations) and distribute through major retailers, which makes demand forecasting, promotional planning, and retailer compliance the central supply chain challenges.

Persona 01 · Economic Buyer

Chief Supply Chain Officer / SVP Supply Chain

Their world

Owns end-to-end supply chain across categories and channels. Core challenge: commercial demand (promotions, ad campaigns, new product launches) is generated without supply knowing. Every cycle, the supply team is surprised. Promotional demand planned commercially without supply input, creating recurring OOS/overstock. Retailer chargebacks are a P&L drain. Legacy systems can’t provide real-time demand signals.

What they care about

  • Supply resilience through promotional demand spikes
  • Reducing working capital in excess inventory
  • End-to-end visibility from supplier to retail shelf
  • Making AI investments pay off, after demand sensing pilots stall
  • Eliminating the gap between commercial planning and supply execution

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Case Fill Rate

    Share of cases ordered by the retailer that were shipped complete from the first shipment.

    Cases shipped ÷ cases orderedCPG best-in-class runs ~98%+
  • OTIF

    On Time In Full: share of orders delivered on the agreed date and in the full quantity. Retailers such as Walmart and Tesco fine suppliers who miss targets.

    Orders on time and in full ÷ total ordersMissed OTIF triggers fines and deductions
  • Inventory Turns

    How many times inventory is sold and replaced over a year. Higher means less cash tied up in stock.

    COGS ÷ average inventory valueInverse of days on hand
  • SC Cost as % Revenue

    Total cost to plan, source, make and deliver product, as a share of net revenue.

    Total supply chain cost ÷ net revenueBoard-level efficiency KPI
  • Cash-to-Cash Cycle

    Days between paying suppliers and collecting cash from customers.

    Days inventory + days receivable − days payableLower frees working capital
  • Forecast Accuracy (MAPE)

    Mean Absolute Percentage Error: average size of forecast error relative to actual demand. Accuracy is usually shown as 1 − MAPE.

    Mean of |actual − forecast| ÷ actualLower MAPE = better forecast

Persona 02 · Economic Buyer

VP / Head of Demand Planning / S&OP Lead

Their world

Owns the demand signal and S&OP alignment. Primary challenge: trade promotion plans built commercially without supply constraint visibility, handed to supply as a done deal. Historical POS from retailers rarely integrated with planning systems. Every promotional cycle creates OOS or overstock waves. S&OP prep consumes most of the week.

What they care about

  • Promotional lift accuracy, eliminating the post-promo void and pre-promo OOS cycle
  • One number everyone agrees on, ending demand vs supply vs finance reconciliation
  • Faster response to demand signals, so market changes land in hours not next week’s batch
  • Accurate forecasting for high-volatility SKUs (NPD, seasonal, promotional lines)
  • Less time building S&OP pack, more time making decisions

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Forecast Accuracy (MAPE / WMAPE)

    MAPE averages percentage error across SKUs equally; WMAPE weights each error by volume, so big sellers count more and tiny SKUs don’t distort the score.

    WMAPE = Σ|actual − forecast| ÷ Σ actualWMAPE is the CPG norm
  • Forecast Bias

    Whether forecasts are consistently too high or too low, regardless of size of error. Persistent bias builds excess stock or causes OOS.

    Σ(forecast − actual) ÷ Σ actualTarget is close to 0%
  • Promotional Lift Accuracy

    How close the forecast uplift for a promotion was to the actual incremental volume sold.

    1 − |actual lift − forecast lift| ÷ actual liftDrives pre-promo build and post-promo dip
  • S&OP Cadence Adherence

    Share of planned S&OP / IBP cycle steps (demand review, supply review, pre-S&OP, exec meeting) held on time with the required inputs.

    Steps held on time ÷ steps plannedProcess discipline indicator
  • Planning Cycle Time

    Elapsed time to produce an agreed plan, from data cut-off to signed-off decision.

    Days from data cut-off to sign-offMost of it is usually data prep
  • Customer Fill Rate

    Share of customer order volume delivered in full, usually measured in cases or order lines.

    Units delivered ÷ units orderedDrives retailer scorecards

    Number of AI/ML models running in live business processes, not pilots or PoCs.

    Time from a business question being asked to a trusted answer being available.

    Share of critical business data that is catalogued, owned, quality-checked and access-controlled.

    Governed critical datasets ÷ total critical datasetsPrerequisite for trusted AI
  • Self-service adoption

    How many business users answer their own questions from governed data without raising IT requests.

    Active self-service users; tickets avoidedProxy for business satisfaction

Persona 04 · Economic Buyer

CPO / VP Procurement

Their world

Owns supplier relationships, ingredient/raw material sourcing, and procurement budget. Primary challenge has shifted from cost management to supply continuity. Ingredient shortages and commodity volatility directly impact production and COGS. Needs real-time visibility into supplier capacity and commodity pricing, but that data lives in fragmented EDI feeds and spreadsheets.

What they care about

  • Ingredient and raw material supply continuity, 6–8 weeks before production impact
  • Real-time commodity price feeds integrated with cost modelling
  • Reducing emergency sourcing costs when shortages hit
  • Supplier sustainability and ESG compliance
  • Working capital impact of procurement decisions

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Supplier OTIF

    On Time In Full measured inbound: share of supplier deliveries that arrive on the agreed date and in full.

    Supplier POs on time and in full ÷ total POsUpstream mirror of retailer OTIF
  • Procurement Cost Savings

    Reduction in spend versus a baseline price or budget, from negotiation, sourcing or demand changes.

    (Baseline price − new price) × volumeClassic CPO scorecard KPI
  • Supply Risk Lead Time

    How far ahead of production impact a supply risk (shortage, supplier failure, price spike) is spotted.

    Days between risk alert and impact dateLonger = more options to mitigate
  • Emergency Sourcing Cycle Time

    Time to secure alternative supply once a shortage hits.

    Days from shortage alert to replacement POExpedites carry premium cost
  • ESG Compliance Rate

    Share of spend or suppliers meeting sustainability and ethical sourcing requirements (e.g. deforestation, Scope 3 reporting).

    Compliant spend ÷ total spendIncreasingly regulated in the EU

Persona 05 · Business Champion

Head of S&OP / IBP Programme Lead

Their world

Leads S&OP or IBP process across commercial, supply, and finance. Primary frustration: no shared platform means demand, supply, and financial numbers never reconcile, so every cycle starts with a data argument. Promotion plans arrive from commercial without supply constraint input. S&OP pack takes most of the week to assemble from 3–4 different systems.

What they care about

  • One version of truth across commercial, supply, and finance
  • Promotional calendar integration into supply planning, so no more surprise demand spikes
  • Faster response to demand changes
  • Self-service analytics, so S&OP questions get answered without IT tickets
  • Less time in data prep, more in decision-making

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • S&OP Cadence Adherence

    Share of planned S&OP / IBP cycle steps (demand review, supply review, pre-S&OP, exec meeting) held on time with the required inputs.

    Steps held on time ÷ steps plannedProcess discipline indicator
  • Consensus Forecast Accuracy

    Accuracy (usually WMAPE-based) of the final forecast agreed in the S&OP meeting, after commercial and supply input.

    1 − WMAPE of consensus forecastMeasures the process, not the model
  • Forecast Value Add (FVA)

    Whether each step of the planning process (statistical model, planner override, sales input) made the forecast better or worse.

    Error of prior step − error after the stepNegative FVA = the step adds noise
  • Planning Cycle Time

    Elapsed time to produce an agreed plan, from data cut-off to signed-off decision.

    Days from data cut-off to sign-offMost of it is usually data prep
  • Plan Attainment

    How closely actual sales, production or shipments matched the agreed S&OP plan.

    Actual ÷ plan, by periodShows whether the plan is credible

Persona 07 · Expansion Persona

Head of Revenue Growth Management

When to engage

RGM typically sits outside the SC decision chain. They generate commercial demand but rarely influence supply fulfilment. The right time to open this conversation is after a CSCO or CIO sponsor has established the data foundation. Once SC and commercial data are unified, RGM is the function that captures the commercial value: trade ROI, promotional effectiveness, and NielsenIQ enrichment.

Their world

Owns trade promotion strategy, pricing architecture, and category planning. Central frustration: trade promotional ROI cannot be measured because retailer sell-through data, base volume, and spend are never unified. Promotional decisions made on instinct. Trade spend is often the second-largest line after COGS (commonly ~20% of gross revenue), yet runs with no systematic measurement.

What they care about

  • Measuring promotional ROI: which promotions generate positive net revenue?
  • Optimising trade investment across customers, categories, and event types
  • Connecting retailer POS sell-through with internal financials to measure true incremental lift
  • Faster post-event analysis
  • Pricing architecture protecting net revenue per unit

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Trade Spend ROI

    Incremental gross profit generated by a promotion or trade investment, relative to what it cost.

    (Incremental gross profit − trade cost) ÷ trade costMany promotions lose money
  • Net Revenue per Case

    Revenue per case after trade spend, discounts and allowances are taken off the list price.

    Net revenue ÷ cases soldCore RGM pricing KPI
  • Promotional Incremental Volume

    Volume sold during a promotion above what would have sold anyway (the baseline).

    Promoted volume − baseline volumeNeeds POS + baseline joined up
  • Category Share Gain

    Change in the brand’s share of total category sales, usually from NielsenIQ or Circana data.

    Brand sales ÷ category sales, period on periodRequires syndicated data
  • Trade Spend as % Revenue

    Total trade investment (promotions, listing fees, allowances) as a share of gross revenue.

    Trade spend ÷ gross revenueCommonly ~20% in CPG
