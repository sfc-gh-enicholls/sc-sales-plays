CATEGORY 01

Demand & Sell-Through Intelligence

Full-Price Sell-Through %
% of Units Sold Before Markdown
Industry avg: 60–70% Target: 80%+
The primary trading KPI for fashion retail. Every point below target means deeper, earlier markdowns to clear the range. For a £500M business, a 5-point improvement in full-price sell-through is worth tens of millions in protected gross margin.

Snowflake unifies store POS, e-commerce, and warehouse data in real time. Cortex Analyst enables "what is sell-through for style X this week?" in seconds, answered from live data, not next Monday's report.

Forecast Accuracy (MAPE)
Mean Absolute Percentage Error, by Style/Size
Style level: avg 35–50% MAPE Target: <20% MAPE
The foundation metric for everything downstream. A 10-point MAPE improvement reduces terminal stock, emergency chase freight, and lost full-price sales by tens of millions. Most trend-sensing AI pilots fail because online behavioural and POS data are fragmented and never unified with planning.

Cortex AI trend models retrain continuously on live signals: POS sell-through, site search/browse behaviour, and external market data from Snowflake Marketplace. Forecast accuracy compounds over time.

Chase Response Time
Signal to Chase Order Placed
Industry avg: 4–6 weeks Target: <2 weeks
A winning style has a narrow window — typically 2–3 weeks — in which chasing more stock is still commercially worthwhile. Every week of delay from signal to order is lost full-price revenue that a competitor captures instead.

Cortex AI flags outperforming styles against plan within hours of the sell-through shift, giving buying teams days rather than weeks of runway to act.

Size Curve Accuracy
Actual vs Planned Size Distribution of Sell-Through
Industry avg: 70–80% match
Getting the total units right but the size mix wrong is one of the most common and least visible causes of both lost sales (out-of-stock in core sizes) and terminal stock (overstock in tail sizes). Size-curve errors compound across every store and channel.

Store-and-channel-level size curve models built on unified POS history detect deviations from plan while there's still time to reallocate.

Online-to-Store Signal Lag
Time for Digital Behavioural Signal to Reach Planning
Industry avg: 2–4 weeks Target: same-day
Site search, browse, and cart behaviour surface trend shifts days or weeks before they show up in store POS. Most retailers can't get that signal to buying and planning fast enough to matter, missing the earliest and cheapest point to react.

Clickstream and POS data unified on Snowflake means the earliest trend signal reaches buying in hours, not weeks.

Return Rate (E-commerce)
Units Returned ÷ Units Sold Online
Fashion e-com avg: 25–35% Target: <20%
Fashion has among the highest return rates in retail, driven largely by size and fit uncertainty. Returns processing, restocking, and lost peak-season selling days all carry real cost, and poor size-guide data is a leading cause.

Unified size/fit and return history on Snowflake powers better size-guide personalisation, reducing size-driven returns at the point of purchase.

Tapestry: 4B+ rows processed daily from 100+ sources to pinpoint markdown candidates at the product level  ·  Urban Outfitters: 2× more data sources ingested at lower cost

CATEGORY 02

Buying & Assortment Intelligence

OTB Accuracy
Actual Sales vs Open-to-Buy Plan
Industry avg error: 20–30% Target: <10% error
OTB is committed 6–9 months ahead of the season with no live read on how the trend is landing. Getting it wrong in either direction is expensive: over-committing drives terminal stock, under-committing loses full-price sales to a competitor.

Cortex AI reforecasts OTB against live sell-through throughout the season, alongside your existing planning tool rather than replacing it.

Markdown as % of Revenue
Forgone Revenue from Discounting
Industry avg: 8–12% of revenue Target: <6%
Markdown depth compounds directly into gross margin. A retailer marking down 35% of range at an average 30% discount depth is forgoing roughly 10.5% of revenue on that stock — the earlier a weak style is flagged, the shallower the markdown needed to clear it.

Cortex AI flags weak sell-through weeks before the trading meeting, so markdown decisions happen early and shallow instead of late and deep.

Terminal Stock %
Unsold Units at Season End ÷ Units Bought
Industry avg: 30–40% Target: <20%
Terminal stock is the clearest signature of a buying and planning process working blind. It's discovered at season end, when the only remaining options — deep clearance, off-price channels, or write-off — are all margin-destructive.

Early terminal-stock risk scoring by style, weeks ahead of season end, gives buying and planning time to act while margin can still be protected.

Vendor OTIF
Vendor On Time In Full Delivery
Industry avg: 80–88% Target: 95%+
Chase orders are only worth placing if the vendor can actually deliver inside the sell-through window. Vendor reliability determines which chase opportunities are real and which are theoretical.

Cortex AI ranks vendor delivery reliability and compliance history, so chase orders route to vendors who can actually deliver in time.

WSSI Cycle Time & Accuracy
Weekly Sales, Stock & Intake — Build Time and Variance to Actual
Typical build: 1–3 days of manual export and reconciliation Target: live, same-day
The WSSI is the central weekly artefact of fashion merchandising — it drives intake phasing, OTB reforecast, and markdown timing. In most retailers it's hand-built in Excel from exports across planning, store, and e-commerce systems, so by the time it's ready the sell-through it describes is already days stale.

Snowflake's governed semantic layer defines sell-through, stock, and intake once, so the WSSI becomes a live view rather than a weekly rebuild. Merchandisers trade off it instead of assembling it.

Chase Order Cycle Time
Sell-Through Signal to Chase Order Placed
Typical: 3–7 days of data assembly
In most fashion retailers, placing a chase order requires manually cross-checking sell-through, vendor capacity, and open-to-buy across three or four different systems. That delay alone can consume most of the window in which chasing still pays.

Snowflake's governed semantic layer ensures buying and planning read from the same defined sell-through and OTB numbers. Cortex Analyst generates the chase recommendation on demand.

Assortment Productivity
Revenue per SKU / Style Count
Varies widely by category
SKU proliferation without a corresponding sell-through gain dilutes buying budget and warehouse capacity across too many low-performing styles. Identifying which styles genuinely earn their shelf space requires unified sell-through data across the full range.

Cortex Analyst: "Which styles in this category are below revenue-per-SKU threshold this season?", answered from live data in seconds.

Under Armour: unified demand and supply chain data supporting faster, more informed buying decisions  ·  Markdown math: 35% of range at 30% average discount depth ≈ 10.5% of revenue forgone

CATEGORY 03

Inventory & Working Capital

Stock Turn
COGS ÷ Average Inventory Value
Fast fashion: 6–10x · Department/luxury: 2–4x
Higher turn means less capital tied up in stock and less exposure to terminal stock risk. Stock turn in fashion is highly sensitive to buying and markdown discipline. A turns improvement of 0.5x on a £300M inventory base frees meaningful working capital.

Unified sell-through and buying signals drive better turns through more accurate inventory positioning by style, store, and channel.

Days of Supply
Inventory on Hand ÷ Average Daily Sales
Varies significantly by category and season
Direct measure of how much capital is locked in inventory at any point in the season. For seasonal fashion product, days of supply should decline predictably as the season progresses; when it doesn't, that's the earliest signal of a terminal-stock problem forming.

Cortex AI-driven sell-through sensing reduces the uncertainty that forces excess days of supply held as a buffer against poor visibility.

Cross-Channel Inventory Accuracy
System-of-Record vs Actual Available Inventory
Industry avg: 85–92% Target: 98%+
When store, warehouse, and online inventory records disagree, retailers either oversell (cancelled online orders, poor customer experience) or undersell (stock sits unavailable to the channel that could move it).

Store POS, warehouse, and e-commerce inventory unified in one governed platform for a single, trusted cross-channel availability view.

Excess & Obsolete Inventory
E&O as % of Total Inventory Value
Industry avg: 8–15% Best-in-class: <4%
E&O in fashion is largely terminal stock discovered too late to chase or discount early. An average £500M fashion retailer writes off a significant share of margin annually to end-of-season clearance and disposal.

Cortex AI detects at-risk terminal stock by style weeks before season end, opening options (early markdown, inter-store transfer) that disappear once the season closes.

Cash-to-Cash Cycle Time
DIO + DSO − DPO
Industry avg: 40–70 days Target: reduce 10–20%
The CFO's primary supply chain metric. Fashion cash conversion is uniquely affected by seasonal buying commitments made months before revenue is realised, so faster sell-through and disciplined markdown directly shorten the cycle.

Snowflake connects trading and financial data in one platform, enabling real-time cash-to-cash tracking and scenario modelling for working capital decisions.

Inventory Carrying Cost
20–30% of Annual Inventory Value
Carrying cost rate: 20–30%/yr
The true cost of holding fashion inventory: capital cost + storage + handling + markdown risk. Reducing average inventory held, without increasing stock-outs, saves meaningfully before even counting the freed working capital.

Better demand/buying matching through unified Snowflake data and Cortex AI directly reduces average inventory held across the season.

Sustainability & Compliance

CSRD Disclosure Completeness
% of Required Disclosures with Auditable Data
Highly variable by retailer maturity
CSRD requires auditable, traceable disclosures on an annual cycle with real regulatory deadlines. Most retailers assemble this manually from supplier PDFs and spreadsheets today, a process that doesn't scale and doesn't satisfy an auditor.

Product, material, and supplier data already unified for trading purposes extends directly into CSRD reporting, with built-in lineage for auditability.

Supplier Traceability Coverage
% of Suppliers with Digitised Material/Origin Data
Industry avg: well under half for most retailers
ESPR and the coming Digital Product Passport mandate require material origin and composition data at the product level. Traceability coverage today is limited mainly by fragmented supplier documentation, not by lack of regulatory clarity.

Compliance scorecards surface directly to buyers during the buying process, building traceability coverage as a byproduct of normal trading, not a separate compliance project.

ESPR / DPP Readiness
Composite Readiness Score Against Enforcement Dates
Enforcement dates fixed by regulation, not by IT capacity
ESPR ecodesign requirements and the Digital Product Passport mandate arrive on a fixed regulatory timetable. Retailers who start building the data foundation now avoid a costly last-minute scramble against a deadline they cannot move.

A governed data foundation built once for trading and buying is the fastest path to ESPR and DPP readiness, reused rather than rebuilt for compliance.

Vendor Compliance Rate
% of Vendors Meeting Sustainability/Compliance Standards
Increasingly a buying gate, not just a checkbox
Sustainability and compliance data is moving from an annual audit exercise to a point-of-decision gate in the buying process itself, which requires it to be available at the moment a buyer places an order, not months later.

Vendor compliance scorecards, built from the same unified supplier data, surface in real time wherever buying decisions happen.
