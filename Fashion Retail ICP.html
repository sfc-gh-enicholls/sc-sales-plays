What is fashion retail?

Brands and retailers that design, buy and sell apparel, footwear and accessories through stores, online and wholesale. Stock is committed months ahead of an uncertain trend and sold in a short full-price window, so the central challenges are reading demand early, chasing winners, placing the right sizes in the right stores and controlling markdown.

Persona 01 · Economic Buyer

Commercial Director / Trading Director / Chief Commercial Officer

Their world

Accountable for the trading result. Signs off OTB, markdown strategy and margin across stores, online and wholesale. Every season is a bet placed months out, and the read on how the range is landing arrives in a weekly pack built from exports. Full-price sell-through, markdown and terminal stock make or break the year, and all three are seen late.

What they care about

  • Seeing how the range is landing by style, size and channel while there is still season left
  • Shallower, earlier markdown instead of deep, late clearance
  • Less terminal stock at season end
  • One trading number for buying, merchandising and finance
  • AI that reaches the trading meeting, not another pilot

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Full-price sell-through

    Share of units bought that sell before any markdown.

    Units sold at full price ÷ units receivedThe primary lever on gross margin
  • Gross margin %

    Sales minus cost of goods, after markdown, as a share of sales.

    (Net sales − COGS) ÷ net salesBoard-level trading number
  • Markdown % of sales

    Value given away in price reductions, relative to sales.

    Markdown £ ÷ net salesDeep, late markdown is the cost of a late signal
  • Terminal stock %

    Stock left at season end that must be cleared, written down or carried over.

    End-of-season units ÷ units boughtAlso a waste and ESPR exposure
  • Sales vs budget

    Actual trading sales against the agreed budget for the period.

    Actual sales ÷ budgeted salesWeekly trading-meeting headline
  • Stock turn

    How many times stock is sold through and replaced in a year.

    Annual sales at cost ÷ average stock at costHigher turn frees cash

Persona 02 · Business Champion

Merchandising Director / Head of Merchandising

Their world

Owns the OTB, depth and size ratios, intake phasing and the WSSI. The OTB is committed months before the season on last year’s numbers and a trend view. Each week the WSSI is rebuilt by hand from planning, e-commerce and store exports, so the team spends its time building the numbers rather than acting on them.

What they care about

  • A WSSI that reflects live sales, stock and intake, not a weekly hand-build
  • Reforecasting OTB in-season against actual sell-through
  • One number that ends the buying vs merchandising vs finance reconciliation
  • Phasing intake to demand so stock lands when it sells
  • More time on decisions, less on the trading pack

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Sales, stock & intake vs WSSI

    Actual weekly sales, stock and intake against the Weekly Sales, Stock and Intake plan.

    Actual ÷ WSSI plan, by week and categoryThe core UK merchandising control
  • OTB accuracy

    How closely the open-to-buy committed matched what the season actually needed.

    1 − |OTB committed − OTB required| ÷ OTB requiredMeasures the bet placed months ahead
  • Weeks cover

    How many weeks current stock will last at the current rate of sale.

    Stock units ÷ average weekly unit salesToo high means markdown risk; too low means lost sales
  • Forecast accuracy (style/size)

    Error between forecast and actual demand at style and size level.

    1 − WMAPEFashion error runs high at size level
  • Markdown % of sales

    Value given away in price reductions, relative to sales.

    Markdown £ ÷ net salesDeep, late markdown is the cost of a late signal
  • Stock turn

    How many times stock is sold through and replaced in a year.

    Annual sales at cost ÷ average stock at costHigher turn frees cash

Persona 03 · Business Champion

Buying Director / Head of Buying

Their world

Selects the range and the vendors, negotiates cost price and, with merchandising, commits the buy. Has limited room to chase a winner or cut a loser once the season starts, because vendor lead times and capacity decide what is still possible. Needs to know early which options are working.

What they care about

  • Seeing winners and losers early enough to chase or cut
  • Intake margin protected through cost price and vendor choice
  • Knowing which vendors can deliver a repeat order on time
  • Range decisions informed by how similar options sold
  • Supplier compliance data at the point of buying

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Range sell-through

    Sell-through for the range or category the buyer selected.

    Units sold ÷ units received, by rangeShows whether the range landed
  • Intake margin

    Planned margin on goods at the point they are bought, before markdown.

    (Full selling price − cost price) ÷ full selling priceSet by buying and cost negotiation
  • Best / worst seller ratio

    Share of options that are clear winners against clear losers in a range.

    Options in top band ÷ options in bottom bandGuides chase and cut decisions
  • Chase lead time

    Days from deciding to repeat a winning style to stock landing in the DC.

    DC receipt date − repeat PO dateDecides whether a chase is still worth it
  • Full-price sell-through

    Share of units bought that sell before any markdown.

    Units sold at full price ÷ units receivedThe primary lever on gross margin

Persona 04 · Business Champion

Supply Chain & Sourcing Director / Head of Supply Chain / Sourcing Director

Their world

Owns suppliers, inbound flow, DCs and delivery. Product is sourced far from market, so supplier delays, freight disruption and duty changes move intake dates and landed cost. PO, shipment and carrier data arrive as files from many partners, so a late shipment is often found when the DC slot is missed.

What they care about

  • Seeing late POs and shipments before they miss the launch window
  • Landed cost that keeps up with freight and duty changes
  • Supplier OTIF and lead-time reliability by vendor and route
  • Carrier and 3PL data without manual file handling
  • Supplier traceability for compliance

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Supplier OTIF

    Share of purchase orders delivered on the agreed date and in the full quantity.

    POs on time and in full ÷ total POsLate intake shortens the full-price window
  • Lead time variability

    Spread of actual supplier and freight lead times around the plan.

    Std. deviation of actual lead time ÷ planned lead timeDrives safety stock and missed launches
  • Chase lead time

    Days from deciding to repeat a winning style to stock landing in the DC.

    DC receipt date − repeat PO dateDecides whether a chase is still worth it
  • Landed cost

    Full cost of a unit delivered to the DC: product, freight, duty and handling.

    Cost price + freight + duty + handlingMoves with freight and duty changes
  • Delivery performance

    Share of customer and store deliveries arriving on the promised day.

    Deliveries on time ÷ total deliveriesLast-mile and store replenishment

Persona 05 · Business Champion

Head of Allocation & Replenishment / Allocation & Replenishment Manager

Their world

Decides where stock goes: initial allocation by store and size, in-season replenishment and transfers. Size curves and store grades are often last year’s, so end sizes pile up in the wrong stores while popular sizes sell out. Every misallocation becomes a transfer or a markdown.

What they care about

  • Size curves and store grades based on current demand
  • Fewer broken size runs in the stores that sell the style
  • Replenishment that reacts to this week’s sales
  • Fewer, cheaper inter-store transfers
  • One stock view across stores, DCs and online

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Allocation accuracy

    How well initial allocation matched where demand actually landed.

    1 − share of stock later moved or marked down for being in the wrong placeBad allocation becomes markdown
  • Availability / lost sales

    Share of time a size is in stock where customers look for it, and the sales lost when it is not.

    In-stock size-location days ÷ total size-location daysBroken size runs kill sell-through
  • Size-curve accuracy

    Match between bought size ratios and the sizes customers actually buy.

    1 − size-mix error vs actual sales mixWrong curves leave end sizes for clearance
  • Inter-store transfers

    Stock moved between stores to fix allocation, and its cost.

    Units transferred ÷ units allocatedA cost of the first allocation being wrong
  • Weeks cover

    How many weeks current stock will last at the current rate of sale.

    Stock units ÷ average weekly unit salesToo high means markdown risk; too low means lost sales

Persona 06 · Technical Validator

CIO / CTO / Data Director

Their world

Under pressure to deliver AI value from trading, product and customer data. The stack typically combines a planning tool, PLM such as Centric or PTC FlexPLM, an ERP or merchandising system, an e-commerce platform and store POS, each holding its own copy of the truth. Earlier data lakes produced data no one trusted. Validates the platform, and becomes the economic buyer on larger transformation deals.

What they care about

  • Governed AI that reaches production

  • Supporting an e-commerce or ERP migration without losing history

Persona 10 · Influencer

Sustainability Director / Head of ESG & Compliance

When to engage

Bring in after a Commercial Director or CIO sponsor has the trading data foundation in place. Product, supplier and stock data are then already unified and can extend into traceability and reporting.

Their world

Owns sustainability reporting and product compliance. For brands selling into the EU, the ESPR ban on destroying unsold apparel and footwear now applies to large companies, and Digital Product Passport requirements for textiles are on the way. CSRD scope was narrowed by the 2025 Omnibus, so check which entities are in scope. Traceability data still lives in supplier PDFs and spreadsheets.

What they care about

  • Traceability for material origin and supplier tiers
  • Evidence for how unsold stock is handled
  • Less terminal stock, which is also a waste metric
  • Supplier compliance data in front of buyers at the point of decision
  • Disclosures that stand up to audit

How they’re measured tap for definition

  • Supplier traceability coverage

    Share of products with material origin and supplier tiers recorded.

    Products with traced tiers ÷ total productsFoundation for a Digital Product Passport
  • Unsold stock disposal

    Volume of unsold goods destroyed, donated, recycled or resold.

    Units by disposal routeNow regulated for large companies selling into the EU
  • Terminal stock %

    Stock left at season end that must be cleared, written down or carried over.

    End-of-season units ÷ units boughtAlso a waste and ESPR exposure
  • Disclosure readiness

    Share of required sustainability data points sourced from systems, not spreadsheets.

    System-sourced data points ÷ required data pointsAudit and assurance burden
