OUR STARTING POINT

The Pharmaceutical Supply Chain is Now the Compliance Function.

Four obligations now sit in your remit that regulators placed there — with dates, penalties, and no opt-out. Each one moved from a legal and quality function into supply chain because the data you hold is the only way to meet them.

Traceability
DSCSA and EU FMD make every batch a compliance obligation

Serialisation and chain of custody for every Rx drug unit from manufacturer to pharmacy dispense. Non-compliance is not a fine. It’s a market withdrawal. Your supply chain data is the only system that can satisfy this mandate.

Quality
Every batch failure is a P&L event and a regulatory filing

GxP / 21 CFR Part 11 mandates data integrity, complete audit trails, and full batch genealogy. A batch rejection without a traceable root cause is not a QA problem — it’s a warning letter waiting to be issued.

Supply Continuity
Patient supply shortages are reported to the FDA

Drug shortages for critical medicines trigger FDA notification requirements. Supply continuity is not a commercial KPI — it’s a regulatory obligation. And it requires multi-channel demand visibility that most pharma companies don’t have.

Cold Chain
Temperature excursions are regulatory events with patient impact

For biologics, vaccines, and cell therapies, cold chain failure = batch loss + regulatory notification + potential patient harm. IoT sensor streams from 3PL partners generate data volumes that existing systems cannot process, let alone detect anomalies in.

What changed

The regulatory mandate extended from QA into supply chain data. Compliance became an infrastructure requirement, not just a quality programme — and it requires your operational data to be governed, traceable, and AI-ready.

What didn’t

Your data infrastructure. QMS, MES, LIMS, ERP, and cold chain IoT were built independently. You are being asked to provide unified, traceable, auditable compliance evidence from systems that have never been connected.
