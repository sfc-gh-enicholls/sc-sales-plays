Supply Chain Conceptual Demo Flow

Automotive Tier 1 scenario — Vectron Components GmbH — Powertrain & Chassis

What we’re doing & how it relates to the play

“Nobody knows their tier-3 exposure until it stops. See it before it does.”

This demo is the conceptual centrepiece for the Discrete Manufacturing “Every Tier, Every Part” play. The play’s premise: every discrete manufacturer can see their own BOM and their tier-1 suppliers. Almost none can see two or three tiers deep — the raw material refiners, the specialty processors, the single-source chokepoints that everything else depends on. That blind spot is not a technology limitation of any one company; it’s a data-sharing problem across company boundaries. Snowflake is the only platform that solves it without anyone having to move, copy, or expose their proprietary data to get there.

Positioning: The Vectron Components scenario shows discrete manufacturing supply chain and procurement leaders how live, governed data sharing across tiers — not EDI integration, not portals, not spreadsheets — turns a multi-tier blind spot into a mapped, quantified, and actionable exposure.

What this demo proves: One narrative event — a new export control on a rare-earth material at a tier-3 refiner Vectron has never directly dealt with — traced up through tier-2 to Vectron’s own BOMs and OEM commitments, using data shared live from tier-2 and tier-3 partners’ own Snowflake accounts. It maps to the play’s arc: Decision Speed (the exposure surfaces automatically, not after weeks of phone calls — S1) → Intelligence Edge (the multi-tier BOM graph and financial exposure are computed, not manually assembled — S2) → Signal to Action (supplier collaboration, alternate sourcing, and shop floor mitigation close the loop — S3–4).

The Narrative Event — A Tier 3 Chokepoint Nobody Tracked

The trigger: A new export licensing requirement on rare-earth permanent magnets takes effect in 4 weeks, originating at Boreal Rare Earth Refining — a tier-3 supplier Vectron has never directly contracted with or even heard of.
The exposure: Boreal’s magnet alloy is a sole-sourced input to the actuator motors that Elyon Sensorik (Vectron’s tier-2 supplier) uses to build active suspension control sensors — a component Vectron buys directly and fits across 4 OEM vehicle programmes.
The scale of the blind spot: Once the exposure is mapped, 2 additional component families — sourced through 3 different tier-2 suppliers — are found to share the same tier-3 chokepoint at Boreal. A concentration risk that no single tier-1 relationship could have revealed.

Section 1 — SC Health & Alerts

Persona: VP Supply Chain / CSCO
Scene 1a · Multi-Tier Network Health
Setup
The VP Supply Chain starts their week in a single control tower dashboard — not just Vectron’s own 12 plants and 6 OEM programmes, but a live health view of the wider supplier network: how many tier-2 and tier-3 suppliers are actively sharing data into Snowflake, and where coverage gaps remain.
What to show
  • Control tower with network coverage KPIs: tier-2 suppliers connected (247 of 300), tier-3 suppliers connected (890 of 1,400+), exposure mapped vs. unmapped BOM value
  • AI-surfaced exceptions: which component families still have zero tier-3 visibility, ranked by revenue exposure
  • Live refresh — supplier-shared data updates continuously via Snowflake Data Sharing, no batch file, no portal login required from either side
Aha moment
Call out: “This isn’t a survey Vectron sent to suppliers once a year. It’s a live network health score — who’s sharing, what’s still dark, and which dark components carry the most risk.”
So what
Outcome: The VP knows where the blind spots are before a disruption hits — not after. Network visibility becomes a managed asset, not a one-off exercise.
Scene 1b · Key Alert — Tier-3 Export Control Signal
Setup
An external trade compliance feed from the Snowflake Marketplace flags a new export licensing requirement on rare-earth magnets, naming Boreal Rare Earth Refining as an affected producer. Vectron has no record of Boreal anywhere in its own ERP or PLM — the connection only exists because Elyon Sensorik, Vectron’s tier-2 supplier, has shared its own upstream exposure data into Snowflake.
What to show
  • AI cross-references the regulatory signal against Elyon’s shared sub-tier exposure data and Vectron’s own BOM — without Vectron ever seeing Elyon’s full supplier list or commercial terms, only the specific exposure that matters to Vectron
  • Alert generated: “Active suspension control sensor (4 OEM programmes) has tier-3 exposure to a magnet alloy now subject to export licensing, effective in 4 weeks”
  • Financial exposure auto-quantified: €38M in annual sensor purchases across the 4 affected programmes
Aha moment
Call out: “Vectron never had a contract with Boreal, never audited them, never even knew their name. The exposure surfaced because Elyon shared a governed slice of their own upstream risk data — not the whole supplier list, just the piece that matters to Vectron. That’s the entire pitch: every tier, every part, without anyone giving up their own data.”
So what
Outcome: A 4-week warning window on a disruption that would otherwise have surfaced as an unexplained sensor shortage from Elyon — with no visibility into why.
Scene 1c · Scenario Comparison
Setup
The VP wants to evaluate mitigation options before escalating to the OEMs. Requalify the sensor with a non-Boreal magnet source? Build buffer stock ahead of the license deadline? Or accept short-term risk while a redesign is evaluated?
What to show
  • Branch from base plan, model each option — scenario recomputes financial and timeline impact instantly
  • Side-by-side comparison: cost, lead time, and OEM OTIF risk for each of the three paths
  • Automated cross-functional task assignment: procurement gets alternate sourcing, production planning gets buffer stock modelling, quality gets requalification scoping
Aha moment
Call out: “Three mitigation paths, modelled and costed, before the 8am stand-up. Six weeks ago this would have started with someone trying to figure out who Elyon’s magnet supplier even was.”
So what
Outcome: A regulatory shock with a 4-week clock becomes a managed decision with weeks of runway, not a scramble.

Section 2 — Multi-Tier Exposure Mapping

Persona: Head of Supply Chain Risk / Supply Planning
Scene 2a · BOM-to-Tier Traceability
Setup
The risk lead needs to trace the full chain: from the finished e-axle assembly, down through Vectron’s own BOM to the sensor, into Elyon’s sub-component structure, to the specific magnet alloy at Boreal — a graph that spans three companies and none of their individual systems.
What to show
  • Multi-tier BOM graph rendered in Streamlit: Vectron’s PLM/ERP BOM data joined live with the specific exposure metrics shared by Elyon (tier 2), which are themselves computed from data shared by Boreal and other tier-3 sources (tier 3) — each party sees only what it needs to
  • Cortex Analyst query: “Which of our finished components rely on magnet alloys subject to the new export licence, and which OEM programmes do they feed?” — answered in seconds from data that spans three companies’ Snowflake accounts
  • No party had to export a spreadsheet, complete a supplier questionnaire, or grant blanket access to their systems — each share is scoped to exactly the fields needed
Aha moment
Call out: “This is a live join across three companies’ data, computed in seconds, and none of them had to give up control of their own data to make it happen. That is not possible with EDI, and it is not possible with a portal.”
So what
Outcome: Multi-tier traceability that used to take a specialist consulting engagement and six weeks now happens as a standing capability, refreshed continuously.
Scene 2b · Network-Wide Concentration Risk
Setup
Having mapped the sensor exposure, the risk lead asks the obvious follow-up: does Boreal’s magnet alloy show up anywhere else in Vectron’s supply base — through completely different tier-2 suppliers that nobody would think to connect?
What to show
  • Network-wide search across all connected tier-2 and tier-3 shares: “Which other components trace back to Boreal, through any supplier path?”
  • Result: 2 additional component families — a steering angle sensor and an e-motor position sensor — sourced through 2 other tier-2 suppliers, both also using Boreal-refined magnets
  • Concentration risk dashboard: total revenue exposure across all 3 component families, and confirmation that this single tier-3 refiner is a hidden chokepoint across 40% of Vectron’s sensor portfolio
Aha moment
Call out: “Three different tier-1 component families, three different tier-2 suppliers, one shared tier-3 chokepoint. Nobody at Vectron would ever have found this by asking their direct suppliers ‘who do you buy from?’ — because none of them would have known to compare notes.”
So what
Outcome: A concentration risk that would otherwise have stayed invisible until three unrelated supply disruptions hit in the same quarter — now visible, quantified, and actionable as one problem.

Section 3 — Supplier Collaboration & Mitigation

Persona: CPO / Procurement Lead
Scene 3a · Tier-2 Supplier Collaboration
Setup
The procurement lead needs Elyon’s help fast — specifically, whether Elyon can requalify the sensor with a non-Boreal magnet source, and how quickly. Rather than a phone call and an email trail, the request and the response both happen inside the same shared data space.
What to show
  • Collaboration workspace in Streamlit: Vectron publishes the specific ask (alternate magnet source options, requalification lead time) directly into a scoped share on Elyon’s Snowflake account
  • Elyon responds by sharing back structured data — two alternate magnet suppliers, requalification timeline, unit cost delta — without disclosing its full supplier contracts or margins
  • Cortex Analyst query on Vectron’s side: “Which of Elyon’s proposed alternates avoid the export licensing requirement entirely?” — answered directly from the shared data
Aha moment
Call out: “This exchange used to take two weeks of emails and NDAs before anyone would share a supplier name. Here, Elyon shares exactly what Vectron needs to make a decision — and nothing more — and it updates in real time as the requalification progresses.”
So what
Outcome: Supplier collaboration compressed from weeks to hours, with both sides retaining control over what they disclose.
Scene 3b · Qualifying an Alternate Tier-3 Source
Setup
Of the two alternate magnet suppliers Elyon proposed, one is EU-based and unaffected by the export licence, at a cost premium. The procurement lead needs a full cost/risk/timeline trade-off for CFO sign-off — covering all 3 exposed component families, not just the sensor.
What to show
  • Trade-off dashboard: cost of switching all 3 component families to the EU-based magnet source vs. cost of production risk if the export licence disrupts Boreal-sourced supply
  • Break-even analysis: expedited requalification cost vs. cost of a line stoppage across the 4 affected OEM programmes
  • One-click approval workflow: decision memo routed to CPO and CFO with full multi-tier exposure context embedded — no separate deck assembled by hand
Aha moment
Call out: “The CFO isn’t approving a supplier switch in isolation — they’re approving a decision that accounts for all three component families this chokepoint touches, with the full financial picture in one place.”
So what
Outcome: A single, well-informed sourcing decision that resolves the concentration risk across the whole portfolio — not three separate scrambles, three months apart.

Section 4 — Production Capacity & Shop Floor

Persona: Head of Production Planning
Scene 4a · Regensburg Plant Impact Modelling
Setup
The Regensburg plant controller needs to know exactly when the sensor requalification needs to land relative to current stock cover, and whether a production slowdown is ever on the table if the timeline slips.
What to show
  • Plant capacity dashboard: live sensor stock cover by line, tied directly to the requalification timeline from Scene 3a — both feeds updating from the same governed data, not two separate reports someone has to reconcile
  • Scenario overlay: requalification on schedule vs. requalification delayed by 2 weeks — system flags the exact week stock cover would run out under each scenario
  • Buffer stock build option: cost and feasibility of building 6 extra weeks of sensor safety stock from remaining Boreal-sourced supply before the licence takes effect
Aha moment
Call out: “The plant team isn’t waiting for a memo from procurement. The requalification timeline and the shop floor stock position are the same data, updating together. If the timeline slips, the plant sees it the same day procurement does.”
So what
Outcome: Production risk managed proactively against a real timeline, not discovered when the sensor bin runs empty on the line.
Scene 4b · Mitigation Plan & Approval Workflow
Setup
All mitigation actions are now aggregated: the alternate magnet source qualification, the buffer stock build at Regensburg, and the portfolio-wide sourcing decision covering all 3 exposed component families. The system assembles these into a single decision pack for sign-off.
What to show
  • Consolidated mitigation dashboard: every action across procurement, risk, and production in one view, each with financial impact, owner, and status
  • Full exposure closure tracker: which of the 3 affected component families are fully resolved, in progress, or still exposed to the export licence deadline
  • One-click approval: decision routed to CSCO and CFO with the complete multi-tier exposure map embedded — the same graph from Scene 2a, now showing mitigated vs. unmitigated paths
  • Post-approval: actions trigger automatically in SAP (buffer stock PO), the qualification workflow with Elyon, and a notification back to the OEM commercial teams confirming supply continuity
Aha moment
Call out: “A disruption that started three companies away from Vectron, at a supplier they’d never heard of, is resolved end to end — discovery, mapping, collaboration, and action — inside four weeks, with a full audit trail for every OEM that asks how it was handled.”
So what
Outcome: Every tier, every part, mapped and actionable — not because Vectron got lucky, but because the data-sharing infrastructure was already in place before the disruption hit.
